SUAAMS checks students into class with their fingerprint and a tap of their phone. Nobody queues at a scanner, and nobody can sign in for a friend.

A 200-student hall shouldn't need a 7-minute queue.

A fingerprint scanner on the door takes about two seconds a student. A phone tap takes under half a second, and the fingerprint check happens on the student's own phone before they reach the door.

~7minto get 200 students through a door scanner
<2minfor the same 200 students with SUAAMS
0.1sfor the phone and reader to exchange the check-in code
10sbefore a check-in code expires, so it can't be reused

Three steps at the door.

The lecturer starts the class from the app or the web portal. Then each student does this:

  1. Confirm it's you

    Press Check in and touch the fingerprint sensor, or enter your phone's PIN. The app does nothing until your phone confirms it's you.

  2. Tap the reader

    Hold your phone to the reader at the door. The phone hands over a one-time code that the server signed a moment earlier. It expires after 10 seconds.

  3. You're marked

    The server checks the code and marks you present, or late if class started more than 10 minutes ago. You get a notification, and your name shows up on the lecturer's live list.

No NFC on your phone? Every reader also broadcasts a short Bluetooth code that changes every 5 seconds, so phones without NFC can check in too. Reader lost its connection? It keeps the taps and sends them when it's back online, and the lecturer confirms them.

Know where you stand before exams.

Every course shows how many classes you've attended out of how many were held, so nobody finds out about a shortfall on exam day.

  • Green at 75% or more
  • Amber from 50% up to 75%
  • Red under 50%

Signing in for a friend doesn't work.

Here's each way someone might try to fake attendance, and what stops it.

A friend uses your phone

The app won't make a check-in code until the phone confirms its owner's fingerprint or PIN. Holding your phone isn't enough.

You sign in on a friend's phone

Your account locks to the first phone you sign in on, and that phone can't hold a second student's account. Moving to a new phone means seeing an admin with your school ID.

A check-in counts for the wrong class

A code only works for the class you're enrolled in that's running right now. You can only be marked once per class.

A code gets passed to someone outside

A code belongs to one student and one class and expires after 10 seconds. A tap also needs the phone within about 4 cm of the reader.

Someone makes up a code

Only the server can sign a code, so it rejects any code that was changed or invented. Each reader also sends its own secret with every request, so a fake reader gets nowhere.

The phone is rooted

When it starts, the app checks whether the phone has been rooted or tampered with. On a phone that fails, it won't check in.

What each person gets.

Students

Check in at the door, see today's classes and the week's timetable, and track attendance course by course.

Lecturers

Start a class from your phone or the portal, watch students arrive live, mark anyone the reader couldn't, and export the register.

Heads of department

See the department's attendance by level, and every student's attendance percentage in one list.

Administrators

Import students, courses and timetables in bulk, manage readers by room, and see every change in the activity log.

Your fingerprint never leaves your phone.

SUAAMS doesn't collect fingerprints at all. Your phone checks your finger itself and only tells the app whether it matched. The server only ever sees attendance records.

Read the full privacy policy →

  • No fingerprint or face data. Nothing biometric is stored or sent anywhere.
  • No location tracking. The tap proves you're at the door, so the app never needs GPS.
  • Only what attendance needs. Your name, matric number, courses, attendance records and a phone ID.
  • Encrypted in transit. The app, readers and portal talk to the server over HTTPS only.
